A process designed so that nothing is assumed.

Most project problems are definition problems that were never caught. This process exists to catch them early — before money, before building, and before anyone is disappointed.

The engagement

Six stages, in order, every time.

Stage 01Conversation

A short discussion about what is happening in the business and where the friction actually is. No pitch, and no obligation. If we are the wrong firm, you will find out here rather than in week three.

Stage 02Definition

The problem, the scope and the outcome are written down. Work starts only when both sides agree on what success looks like. The written scope includes what is explicitly out of scope, because that is where projects drift.

Stage 03Confirmation

Scope, price, timeline and payment terms are confirmed in writing before any money changes hands. If the quotation and the scope disagree, the scope wins.

Stage 04Build

The work is delivered in visible stages, with the reasoning explained rather than hidden behind progress updates. You see working software or a working document at each stage, not a status email.

Stage 05Review

You review the work against the agreed scope. Anything that misses the mark is corrected before handover. What is in scope and what is deliberately left out are both written down.

Stage 06Handover

You receive the deliverable, the documentation, the accounts and the credentials, plus a walkthrough — so the system can be run without us. Ownership transfers completely; there is no dependency by design.

After handover

Support is available, and it is optional.

Most clients keep a small monthly retainer for monitoring, workflow adjustments and improvements — usually a few hours a month with a response time agreed in writing.

Some take the documentation and run the system themselves. That is a perfectly good outcome, and we will hand over cleanly either way. Retainers are month to month, with 30 days' notice, and we will not hold your system hostage to a contract.

  • IncludedMonitoring upkeep, workflow changes, small improvements, agreed response time, monthly review of what the automation actually returned.
  • Not includedNew features, new integrations, or scope that was never agreed. Those are quoted separately, in writing, before work begins.
  • Notice30 days, either side, no exit fee, and a full handover of whatever is live at that point.
  • OwnerEvery retainer has a named person responsible for it, and an escalation path if they are unavailable.

Commercial terms

Stated plainly, before you ask.

  • Audit pricePKR 3,000, fixed. Published openly, no tiers, no “from”.
  • Payment — audits100% in advance. Work starts once payment is confirmed as received.
  • Payment — projects50% before work begins, with the balance agreed in writing before delivery.
  • Delivery — auditWithin 72 hours of confirmed payment.
  • Delivery — projectsAgreed in writing per project. We do not commit to a date before the scope is understood.
  • AdjustmentsA seven-day window after an audit is delivered, to correct anything factually wrong about your business.
  • Changes to scopeQuoted separately in writing. We do not absorb silent scope creep, and we do not spring charges on you.
  • ImplementationAlways quoted separately. The audit fee is not credited against a build.

Payment is confirmed by checking that funds have actually been credited, not from a screenshot alone. This protects both sides.

Boundaries

What we will not do

  • Access private data unnecessarily. Audits use public information and whatever you choose to provide. We do not ask for passwords.
  • Promise specific outcomes. Results depend on your process and your market. We will not quote a percentage improvement we cannot stand behind.
  • Present a prototype as finished. A demonstration is labelled a demonstration. A prototype is not sold as a production system.
  • Claim work we have not done. No invented clients, testimonials, case studies, awards or metrics — on our site or yours.
  • Sell work that is not needed. Sometimes the right answer is to do nothing yet. We will say so.

What the work looks like in practice

Every stage has a matching artefact you can hold: a written scope, a quotation, a staging plan, a review checklist, a handover pack. You are never asked to trust a status update — you are shown the thing itself.
